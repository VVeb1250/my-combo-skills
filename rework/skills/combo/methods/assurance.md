# Assurance: review and verify

Identify target revision, accepted behavior and the scope of review or verification. Inspect the real artifact, relevant callers, tests and ownership, not just a diff or completion report. Review on two axes: correctness/safety and change quality (causal ownership, simplicity, valid compatibility, testability). Prioritize concrete defects and consequential risk over style. Every finding needs a plausible reachable case, affected path/line or evidence, consequence and proportionate repair; distinguish inference from reproduced failure. Read-only requests do not authorize fixes.

Select evidence that can falsify each material claim: public behavior for logic, actual UI state transitions/persistence for interaction, current renders with relevant views for visual fidelity, integrated generation on representative seeds for emergent systems, comparable measurement for performance, direct sources for research. File existence, compilation, test count and self-report are proxies. Test both positive and negative paths where the contract requires it. Run explicit gates; reuse applicable results only if code, inputs and environment are unchanged in relevant ways. If changed after a check, reassess its validity.

When a shortcut, filter or custom interface supplies material evidence, check its applicability and fidelity against independent primary evidence. Preserve exit/failure signals, relevant ordering and meaningful omissions; broaden or inspect raw evidence when needed. A compact report is not proof that excluded cases passed. Measure efficiency only against an equivalently accepted outcome.

For scoped completion, inspect task-owned changes and affected contracts, Wiki claims, tests and checkpoint state. Do not confuse a commit with verification. For user taste decisions distinguish objective gaps from pending human judgment. Report exact checks and outcomes, what was not verified and why; do not claim overall acceptance with a material gap. Stop when the authorized outcome is proven, not after an unnecessary second full audit.
