# Research and decisions

Answer the stated question at the requested depth, using supplied material when
it defines the scope. Read [context](../practices/context.md) once for evidence
selection and reporting.

Define the decision and claims that could change it. Search for primary evidence
and consequential counterevidence; compare directness, methods, incentives, date,
and applicability. Several articles repeating one source are not independent
confirmation. Separate observed facts, estimates, and inference; retain units
and assumptions for numerical claims.

For scientific work, distinguish design, population, outcome, and causal limits.
For market sizing, reconcile top-down and bottom-up assumptions. Consult the
[research index](../references/library/research.md) only for an unresolved domain
method, such as a systematic search protocol or patent screening. Adapt the
relevant section rather than adopting an upstream review pipeline by default.

Verify changing facts, uncertain technical claims, and high-stakes guidance with
authoritative current sources using native search/document tools. Native Deep
Research applies only under its own invocation conditions. References grant no
permission to install a search stack or submit user data to a service.

Stop searching when material claims have adequate support and consequential
contradictions are resolved or disclosed. Systematic/exhaustive requests still
require their specified search and screening coverage. An inaccessible source
or empty search is a coverage gap, not proof that no evidence exists.

Deliver the answer with citations beside claims. For decisions, recommend one
option and identify evidence that would change it. Keep material gaps; omit
search-history narration and universal risk checklists.
